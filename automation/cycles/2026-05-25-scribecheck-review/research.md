# Research Notes: ScribeCheck Review

## Sources reviewed

- JAMA Network Open, 2025: Ambient Documentation Technology in Clinician Experience of Documentation Burden and Burnout. The study covered clinicians at Mass General Brigham and Emory and reported lower burnout measures after ambient documentation use, while free-text comments still surfaced editing burden, note style issues, integration drawbacks, and specialty-fit problems. Source: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2837847
- AMIA TrendBurden, 2026: AMIA continues to track excessive documentation burden across health professionals, with the Spring 2026 survey open and annual administration beginning in 2026. Source: https://amia.org/about-amia/amia-25x5/trendburden-pulse-survey
- arXiv, 2025: Patient Safety Risks from AI Scribes: Signals from End-User Feedback. The abstract reports safety concerns from AI scribe user feedback, especially transcription errors involving medication and treatment. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04118

## Signal

AI scribes are useful, but the review experience is under-designed. Clinicians do not only need drafts. They need fast confidence about what to trust, what to check, and what to edit.

## Chosen problem

Post-generation review of AI clinical notes is still cognitively expensive and safety-sensitive.

## Target user

Telehealth clinicians, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, and clinical reviewers who must finalize notes after AI-assisted documentation.

## Product angle

Do not build another AI scribe. Build the clinician-facing review layer that makes AI output inspectable, editable, and accountable.
